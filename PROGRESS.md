## 2026-09-11 — Live upcoming-race prediction added; extending data through 2025-2026

- **`src/models/live_predict.py`** — predicts a real upcoming race rather than replaying a historical one. Reuses everything: pulls the historical raw table, appends one live race's worth of rows (built from whatever real FastF1 session data exists for it right now — FP1-3, qualifying), then runs the exact same circuit/driver/team/relative/strategy feature layers (`build_dataset.build()`, refactored to accept a pre-assembled `raw` table) and the same trained model. No separate live model, no duplicated feature math, no fixed "stage" enum — a session that hasn't happened yet fails to load and its columns stay `NaN` natively, same mechanism as `predict.py`'s historical `mask_for_stage` check from Phase 2.
- **Grid position and starting compound are the two columns that can never be filled in early**, no matter how much practice/quali data exists, because they genuinely don't exist until qualifying fully resolves (grid, including penalties) and race day (compound). `live_predict.py` uses qualifying classification as a grid proxy pre-penalties and accepts `grid_overrides`/`compound_overrides`/`lineup_overrides` for known real values.
- **Real-world test against Madrid (2026 round 14, this week's race, zero sessions run yet)** confirmed the whole pipeline end to end — produced a full 20-driver ranking using only historical form + circuit reference, correctly leaving grid/quali/practice as `NaN`. Output quality is currently limited by stale driver-lineup data (pulled from the most recent race in our processed dataset, which was Abu Dhabi 2024) — exactly what the in-progress 2025-2026 backfill (below) fixes.
- **Circuit reference table fixes needed to even reach 2025-2026 data:** added `LOCATION_ALIASES` to `circuit_reference.py` (Monaco -> "Monte Carlo", Miami -> "Miami Gardens", Yas Island -> "Yas Marina" — FastF1 renamed these location strings for the same physical circuits starting around 2026; the resolver would otherwise raise on them) and a hand-curated **Madrid** row (`circuit_reference.csv`, `valid_from_year=2026`) since it's a brand-new calendar addition with zero prior rows. Madrid's values are flagged as a rougher inaugural-circuit estimate than the other 25 (no real race history behind it yet).
- **`src/features/build_dataset.py`'s `build()` now accepts an optional pre-loaded `raw` DataFrame** instead of always reading from disk — the one change that makes reusing the full training-time feature pipeline for live prediction possible without duplicating any of it.
- **Project plan updated**: documented live-upcoming-race prediction as an explicit added capability (not silently smuggled into Phase 2's original scope), added car-upgrade tracking to the "Future feature extensions" table (deferred — team_recent_form/team_race_pace do eventually reflect an upgrade via recency-weighted lag, but the race an upgrade debuts at is currently a real blind spot; fixing it needs a hand-curated upgrade calendar, which doesn't exist in FastF1/Jolpica).
- **In progress (background):** `python -m src.data.ingest --seasons 2025 2026` extending the dataset through the most recent completed race (Monza 2026, round 13) — same rate-limit-paced backfill as the original Phase 1 pull. Once done: rebuild `model_matrix.parquet`, retrain the Phase 2 model on the fuller/fresher dataset, and re-run `live_predict.py` against Madrid to confirm the lineup/form staleness issue is resolved.
- **Known gap not yet addressed:** round 16 of the 2026 calendar is listed as "Bahrain Grand Prix" at location "Kuala Lumpur" — genuinely not in the circuit reference table and not an alias for an existing row. Not blocking anything right now (that race is months away and won't be hit by ingestion until it's actually happened), but will need its own row before it's reachable.

## 2026-09-11 — Phase 2 complete: finishing-position predictor trained and evaluated

- **Training** (`src/models/train_finish_position.py`, `src/models/features.py`): XGBoost regressor (`reg:absoluteerror`) on the Phase 1 model matrix, single model, no per-circuit/per-cluster variants. Feature prep adds one interpretable interaction column (`grid_position × overtaking_difficulty`, per the plan's suggestion) and encodes `starting_tire_compound` as a **fixed-category** categorical (`SOFT/MEDIUM/HARD/INTERMEDIATE/WET`) rather than inferring categories per-batch — needed so a single-row rolling prediction with the compound not yet known doesn't end up with zero known categories and crash XGBoost's categorical split logic.
- **Time-based CV, not random splits:** `time_based_splits()` builds 5 expanding-window folds over chronologically-sorted *races* (via `sklearn.TimeSeriesSplit` on unique (season, round) pairs, then mapped back to rows) — every fold's test set is strictly later than its train set, and all ~20 drivers in one race always land on the same side of a fold (never split across train/test). `RandomizedSearchCV` (40 iterations) tunes over this same fold set — sklearn's own CV machinery was reused rather than hand-rolling a tuner or reaching for Optuna (an unneeded new dependency on top of already-required scikit-learn).
- **Results:** baseline (grid position = finish position) MAE **3.48** positions vs tuned model MAE **3.04** — a genuine ~0.44-position improvement, in line with how strong a predictor grid position already is in real F1. Calibration table (predicted vs actual by quintile on the held-out last fold) tracks the diagonal closely. Metrics + best hyperparams saved to `src/models/saved/finish_position_metrics.json`; model itself to `src/models/saved/finish_position_xgb.json` (both gitignored — added `src/models/saved/` to `.gitignore` since the pre-existing `models/saved/` pattern is root-anchored and doesn't match the nested path).
- **SHAP sanity check (the plan's explicit ask):** confirmed — mean |SHAP| of `grid_x_overtaking_difficulty` is **0.32 at hard-to-overtake tracks** (overtaking_difficulty ≥ 0.7, e.g. Monaco/Hungaroring/Zandvoort-like) vs **0.18 at easy ones** (≤ 0.3, e.g. Monza/Spa-like) — circuit characteristics really do matter more where the plan predicted they should. Top SHAP features overall: `driver_recent_form`, `team_recent_form`, `grid_position`, `historical_compound_performance`, `quali_gap_to_pole` — a sensible ranking.
- **Rolling re-prediction** (`src/models/predict.py`): one model, called again with progressively more real session data — `mask_for_stage()` blanks out not-yet-known columns (grid/quali-derived at `post_practice`, starting compound at `post_quali`) rather than requiring separate per-stage models, since XGBoost's native missing-value handling does the work. Verified in `tests/test_rolling_predict.py`: predictions genuinely change stage to stage on a real held-out row (7.22 → 4.39 → 4.05 in one sample run) rather than ignoring the new information.
- **Self-checks:** `python tests/test_features.py` (Phase 1, still 4/4) and `python tests/test_rolling_predict.py` (Phase 2, new) both pass. Re-run `python -m src.models.train_finish_position` any time `data/processed/model_matrix.parquet` changes — it retrains and overwrites the saved model/metrics.
- **Status:** Phase 2 done — one working, tuned, evaluated, track-aware, session-updatable model for finishing position.
- **Next:** Phase 3 — add the quali-to-race delta model (same pipeline, same feature table, `target_quali_to_race_delta` already present in the model matrix), and SHAP explainability for both models together.

## 2026-09-11 — Phase 1 complete: data foundation built and backfilled

- **Ingestion** (`src/data/fastf1_client.py`, `src/data/ingest.py`): pulls one row per driver per race (grid, quali gap-to-pole + raw Q1/Q2/Q3 times, FP1-FP3 practice pace, race weather, race outcome/pace/pit stops/starting compound) from FastF1 for seasons 2022-2024, resumable per-race parquet cache in `data/raw/races/`. Full backfill done: **68 races, 0 skipped** (22 rounds 2022, 22 rounds 2023, 24 rounds 2024).
- **Circuit reference table** (`src/features/circuit_reference.csv` + `.py`): 25 circuits, hand-curated proxy values for the 10 v1 columns, keyed by circuit + configuration era (Singapore split into `pre_2023`/`post_2023` to demonstrate the era mechanism — the only layout change inside the 2022-2024 window).
- **Feature layers** (`src/features/{rolling,driver_features,team_features,relative_features,strategy_features}.py`), merged by `build_dataset.py` in the plan's circuit → driver → team → relative → weather/strategy order into `data/processed/model_matrix.parquet`: **1359 rows × 41 columns (33 features + 2 targets + 6 identifiers)**, matching the ~30-45 column spec.
- **Leakage rule applied throughout:** every "form"/historical feature (`src/features/rolling.py`: `recent_form`, `track_form`) is computed only from strictly-prior races via per-group expanding weighted averages — recency-weighted (race-count decay for general form, year-gap decay ~0.7/~0.49 for track form, matching the plan's example). Actual-race-outcome data (finish position, race pace, pit stops) never appears in a same-race feature, only in historical rolling form and in the target columns; pre-race-session actuals (grid position, quali gap, practice pace, starting compound) are used directly since they're genuinely known before the race.
- **Self-check:** `tests/test_features.py` (assert-based, `python tests/test_features.py`) — all 4 checks pass against the real built dataset, including the leakage checks and the shape check (33 feature columns, in the 25-50 expected range).
- **Data quality spot-check:** `target_finish_position` in [1, 20] as expected; the highest-null feature is `driver_track_form` at 43.7% NaN, which is the correct cold-start behavior (a circuit is visited once/season, so a driver needs a *prior year* at that exact circuit — 2022 rows and any circuit's first-ever appearance in the window necessarily have no history yet). All other features are under ~7% null. XGBoost (Phase 2) handles NaN natively, so these are left as-is rather than imputed.
- **Blocker hit and fixed:** FastF1 self-enforces a ~500-calls/hour courtesy rate limit on its free public data sources.
  1. First attempt burned through it in ~7 min with no backoff, silently skipping most races — fixed by making `fastf1_client.load_session` retry-on-`RateLimitExceededError` instead of failing the race.
  2. The retry backoff was initially a flat 60s sleep per attempt, which badly under-uses the real refill rate (~1 call/7.2s) once a race needs many genuinely-fresh calls (e.g. FP2/FP3 never touched before) — a session could stall 40+ minutes making only ~1 call of progress per minute. Fixed by dropping the backoff to 9s (just above the natural refill interval) and raising the attempt cap to keep the same ~3h ceiling. This is tuning our own use of the legitimately allowed rate, not an attempt to bypass the limit — restarting the process to reset FastF1's in-memory counter and get a fresh burst was deliberately avoided as against the spirit of the library's own courtesy throttle.
  3. Practice pace briefly went FP1-only (~40% fewer calls/race) to fit the original slow backoff; reverted to full FP1-FP3 once the backoff was fixed, per explicit request to keep the fuller practice signal and full Q1/Q2/Q3 quali data.
- **Status:** Phase 1 done — data foundation, circuit reference table, and full leakage-safe feature pipeline are built, backfilled, and passing self-checks.
- **Next:** Phase 2 (predictor v1) — train the race-finishing-position XGBoost model on `data/processed/model_matrix.parquet` with time-based CV and hyperparameter tuning.
