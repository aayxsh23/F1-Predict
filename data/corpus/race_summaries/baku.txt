Baku City Circuit — Circuit Notes

Baku combines a tight, twisting old-city section with the longest straight on the calendar (≈2,200m) running alongside the Caspian Sea — one of the most unusual layout combinations in Formula 1. That long straight, feeding into a hard-braking zone, is a genuine overtaking opportunity, which keeps overtaking_difficulty at a moderate level (≈0.35) despite how unforgiving the narrow old-town section is if a driver gets it wrong.

Baku has the highest safety_car_frequency in the entire reference table (≈0.7) and one of the highest DNF rates (≈0.18) — the combination of very high top speed on the straight and wall-lined, low-run-off corners in the castle section means incidents here are frequent and tend to require full safety car neutralization rather than resolving under local yellow flags. The circuit's braking-zone count is low (only 4) relative to its length, which is part of why it doesn't behave like a typical technical street track — most of the lap is either flat-out or a single hard braking event, rather than the constant direction changes of a track like Singapore.

Tyre degradation is on the lower side (≈2/5), since the layout doesn't put sustained lateral load through the tyres the way a flowing permanent circuit does — most of the stress here is longitudinal, from the very hard braking zones rather than cornering load.
