Sepang International Circuit, near Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. A permanent, purpose-built racing circuit, not a street circuit -- it has none of a street track's temporary barriers or public-road surface. Track length 5.543 km, 15 corners, 56 race laps. Formula 1 raced here as the Malaysian Grand Prix from 1999 to 2017; in 2026 it returns to host the Bahrain Grand Prix. None of this project's 2022-2026 data comes from Sepang, so every circuit value for it is an estimate from the layout and its earlier history, and should be revisited once a race has run.

Overtaking is relatively easy (difficulty ≈0.35): two long straights, the start-finish straight and the back straight, each end in heavy braking into a slow corner (Turn 1 and the Turn 15 hairpin), and the back straight into Turn 15 is the classic passing place. Cars can follow through the fast sweepers of the first sector less well.

Tyre degradation is high (≈4 on the 1-5 scale): an abrasive surface, long high-speed corners and track temperatures often above 50°C wear tyres quickly, which historically made two-stop races common.

Weather is the big unknown. Afternoon tropical thunderstorms are frequent (rain chance ≈0.35 in the reference table), heat and humidity make it one of the most physically demanding races for drivers, and the heat stresses brakes and power units, which is why its retirement rate is estimated above average (≈0.15). Safety cars have been moderately common (≈0.4), often weather-related.
