Autódromo José Carlos Pace (Interlagos, São Paulo) — Circuit Notes

Interlagos is a short, anti-clockwise permanent circuit (one of the few anti-clockwise tracks on the calendar, which creates unusual neck-load demands compared to most other rounds) with significant elevation change across the lap. Overtaking difficulty is on the easier side (≈0.3) — the start/finish straight into Turn 1 (the Senna S) is one of the most reliable overtaking spots in Formula 1, helped by the long uphill approach that makes slipstreaming effective.

Rain is a major factor here (rain_race_frequency ≈ 0.35, tied with Spa for one of the highest in the reference table) — São Paulo's local climate produces sudden, intense tropical storms, and mixed or fully wet Interlagos races (including several famous title-deciding ones) are a recurring feature of the calendar rather than an exception. Safety car frequency (≈0.4) and DNF rate (≈0.12) are both moderately elevated, consistent with a short, technical lap where a rain-affected session compounds the normal risk of the technical Senna S and Turn 1 braking zone.

Given the combination of easy overtaking and frequent rain, Interlagos is one of the calendar's least "grid position is destiny" circuits — both the layout and the weather independently push toward more mixed-up, less predictable finishing orders than the average round.
