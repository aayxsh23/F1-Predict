Jeddah Corniche Circuit — Circuit Notes

Jeddah is one of the fastest street circuits ever built for Formula 1 — narrow, wall-lined, and taken at very high average speed despite being a temporary/semi-permanent street layout (is_street_circuit = 1). That combination is precisely why it carries one of the highest safety-car frequencies in the calendar (safety_car_frequency ≈ 0.6): walls punish even small errors, and at the speeds this track is run, an off-line moment easily becomes a barrier contact rather than a spun car that rejoins under its own power. DNF rate is correspondingly elevated (≈0.15).

Overtaking difficulty sits in the middle of the calendar (≈0.55) — there are genuine passing zones into the long back-straight braking area, but the blind, high-speed corners elsewhere on the lap punish a driver who commits to a move without a clear picture of what's ahead, so overtakes are riskier to attempt than the number alone suggests. Tyre degradation is comparatively low (≈2/5) for a track run at such high average speed, since the layout favours flowing, low-slip-angle corners over the sustained lateral loading that chews through tyres elsewhere.

Because Jeddah is a street track, track evolution across the weekend is significant — the surface rubbers in a lot between first practice and qualifying, which is part of why practice pace here is a noisier predictor of race pace than at a permanent circuit with a more stable surface.
