Suzuka International Racing Course — Circuit Notes

Suzuka is one of the most technically demanding circuits on the calendar, famous for its figure-eight layout (the only crossover circuit in F1) and the high-speed Esses/130R sequence. Overtaking difficulty sits at a moderate level (≈0.5) — the hairpin and the final chicane both offer real passing chances, but the flowing middle sector is unforgiving of a car that isn't fundamentally quick, so grid position still carries meaningful weight even where a pass is technically possible.

Tyre degradation is rated at the top of the scale (≈4/5) — the sustained high-speed direction changes through the Esses put some of the highest lateral load on tyres anywhere on the calendar. Rain is a significant factor (rain_race_frequency ≈ 0.3) given Suzuka's typical autumn date and Japan's typhoon-season weather patterns — wet-weather Suzuka races, including red-flagged sessions, are a recurring feature of the calendar rather than a rare event.

Safety car frequency (≈0.3) and DNF rate (≈0.1) are both moderate, reflecting a circuit that rewards precision and punishes a lack of it, without being as consistently incident-prone as a street circuit. Suzuka's combination of high degradation and moderate overtaking difficulty makes tyre strategy and pit-stop timing a particularly important explanatory factor here relative to pure grid position.
