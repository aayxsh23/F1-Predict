Circuit Gilles Villeneuve (Montréal) — Circuit Notes

Montréal is a semi-permanent circuit on Île Notre-Dame, built around a mix of long straights and tight chicanes, with a famous wall at the final corner exit (the "Wall of Champions") that has ended more than one championship contender's race. It carries the shortest pit-lane loss time in the entire reference table (≈15.5s) — the pit lane here is unusually short relative to lap distance — which meaningfully changes the math on whether an extra pit stop is worth attempting compared to almost any other circuit on the calendar.

Overtaking difficulty is moderate (≈0.35), helped by long straights into hard-braking chicanes, but the circuit has the highest DNF rate in the reference table (≈0.2) and a high safety car frequency (≈0.8, second only to Marina Bay) — the combination of high speed, concrete walls very close to the racing line, and frequently changeable weather (rain_race_frequency ≈ 0.2, one of the higher values outside of Spa/Silverstone/São Paulo) makes this one of the most unpredictable, caution-heavy rounds of the season.

Given the low pit-loss cost and high safety-car frequency, race-winning strategy at Montréal is disproportionately about being ready to react to a caution period rather than executing a pre-planned stint plan — a driver's realistic finishing position here is less "locked in" by qualifying than the raw overtaking-difficulty number alone would suggest.
