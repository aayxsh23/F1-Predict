Hungaroring (Budapest) — Circuit Notes

The Hungaroring is a tight, twisty permanent circuit often described as "Monaco without the walls" — there's more run-off than a street circuit, but the layout offers almost no long straights and a constant sequence of medium-speed corners that make following (let alone passing) another car extremely difficult. Overtaking difficulty is rated near the top of the calendar (≈0.8, just behind Zandvoort and well behind only Monaco) — this is one of the clearest examples of a permanent circuit behaving like a street track in terms of how much grid position dictates the finishing order.

Because the layout is so technical and low-speed relative to the rest of the calendar, DNF rate is among the lowest in the reference table (≈0.08) — there simply aren't many high-consequence corners to crash out of, races here tend to be processional and low-drama by F1 standards. Tyre degradation is moderate (≈3/5), and rain is a real if not dominant factor (≈0.1) given the mid-summer Central European date.

Because overtaking is so hard here, qualifying performance is an especially strong predictor of finishing position — a driver who has a bad Saturday at the Hungaroring has fewer realistic paths back through the field than almost anywhere else on the calendar besides Monaco.
