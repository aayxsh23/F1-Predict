Circuit de Monaco — Circuit Notes

Monaco is the single hardest circuit on the calendar to overtake at (overtaking_difficulty ≈ 0.95, the highest value in the reference table by a clear margin) — the track is barely wider than the cars in several places, run through the streets of the Principality with barriers immediately off the racing line and essentially no realistic passing zone under green-flag racing. This is the canonical example of a circuit where grid position is close to destiny: a driver who qualifies well is very likely to finish close to where they started, and a driver who qualifies poorly has almost no way to recover through overtaking alone (track position gained via pit-stop strategy or a safety car becomes disproportionately valuable instead).

Because passing is so difficult, the track's tyre degradation is rated the lowest in the calendar (≈1/5) — cars aren't fighting for position lap after lap the way they do at an overtaking-friendly circuit, so tyre life is rarely the limiting factor in a Monaco strategy the way pure track position is. The circuit is also the shortest on the calendar (3.337 km), meaning more laps are needed to cover full race distance, which raises the cumulative chance that a red flag, safety car, or VSC period (safety_car_frequency ≈ 0.6) reshuffles the strategic picture at some point in the race.

Monaco is the single clearest real-world illustration of why a model needs a grid-position-times-overtaking-difficulty interaction feature: the same grid position is worth far more here than at an easy-overtaking track like Monza.
