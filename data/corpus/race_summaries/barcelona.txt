Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya — Circuit Notes

Barcelona was, for decades, F1's primary pre-season testing venue, so teams historically know this track's characteristics better than almost anywhere else on the calendar — a fact that shows up in how predictable the competitive order tends to be here relative to a novel street circuit. It's a permanent circuit with a demanding mix of high-speed and medium-speed corners, and it's rated among the higher end of the tyre-degradation scale (≈4/5) — the long, high-load corners (particularly the final sector's sustained cornering) put significant lateral stress through the tyres over a race distance.

Overtaking difficulty is moderate-to-high (≈0.65) — there is a DRS zone into Turn 1, but the technical middle sector offers few genuine passing chances, so track position gained in the first few corners tends to hold. Safety car frequency is the lowest in the entire reference table (≈0.2), and DNF rate is similarly low (≈0.1) — this is a circuit with generous run-off and a layout that punishes pace more than it punishes small mistakes, so races here tend to run green from start to finish more often than almost anywhere else on the calendar.

Because degradation is high but overtaking is hard, strategy (undercut/overcut timing) tends to matter more here than outright overtaking on-track — a well-timed pit stop can gain track position in a way that a wheel-to-wheel move often can't.
