Bahrain International Circuit (Sakhir) — Circuit Notes

Bahrain International Circuit is a permanent desert facility, not a street circuit, and it sits at the easy end of the overtaking-difficulty spectrum (overtaking_difficulty ≈ 0.25, tied for the lowest in the calendar alongside Spa). Its long straights (up to ~1,100m) feed into wide, heavy-braking corners with multiple lines through the apex, which is exactly the geometry that makes passing straightforward relative to a street track. This is a big part of why grid position tends to matter less here than at a track like Monaco — a driver who qualifies poorly still has real chances to recover position during the race.

The abrasive desert surface and sustained high-speed cornering load make this one of the higher tyre-degradation circuits on the calendar (tyre_degradation_level ≈ 4/5), so strategy and stint management carry real weight in the finishing order, not just one-lap pace. Safety car interventions are moderate (≈0.3 frequency) — the run-off areas are generous by modern standards, which keeps incident-triggered cautions from being as constant as on a street track, but the fast corners and high tyre wear still produce enough incidents to matter. Rain is essentially a non-factor here (rain_race_frequency ≈ 0.0) given the desert climate, so weather-driven strategy swings that show up at wetter European rounds don't apply at Bahrain.

Because the season often opens here, Bahrain also tends to be a track where pre-season form is least tested by in-season development — form features reflect the prior year's competitive order more directly than at later rounds.
