Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez (Mexico City) — Circuit Notes

Mexico City's defining physical characteristic isn't captured directly in the standard reference-table columns but shapes everything about how the circuit behaves: it sits at roughly 2,200m altitude, among the highest of any F1 venue, where thinner air reduces both aerodynamic downforce and engine power, and teams run cars with unusually high wing levels to compensate. This is part of why overtaking difficulty is moderate (≈0.45) despite a long back straight into the stadium section's hard-braking Turn 1 — cars carry more drag than usual, which both aids slipstreaming on the straight and costs pure top speed.

The circuit has one of the shorter pit-lane loss times outside Montréal (≈18s), and DNF rate and safety car frequency are both fairly average (≈0.1 and ≈0.4). Tyre degradation is on the lower side (≈2/5) — the reduced grip and load from thin air actually means less absolute stress on the tyres than the corner speeds alone would suggest at sea level.

The final stadium section, run through a packed grandstand inside the old baseball stadium, is one of the most atmospheric and overtaking-relevant parts of the lap — cars are close together and slow enough there for genuine wheel-to-wheel racing even without a formal DRS zone.
