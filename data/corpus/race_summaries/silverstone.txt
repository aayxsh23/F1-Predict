Silverstone Circuit — Circuit Notes

Silverstone is one of the fastest permanent circuits on the calendar, built on a former WWII airfield, with a sequence of high-speed corners (Maggotts, Becketts, Chapel) that are among the most demanding in Formula 1. Despite that speed, overtaking difficulty is on the lower side (≈0.35) — the long Hangar Straight into the Stowe/Vale braking zone, plus the Wellington Straight, both create real passing opportunities that offset the technical middle sector.

Tyre degradation is rated at the top of the scale (≈4/5, tied with Bahrain/Barcelona/Spa/Suzuka) — the sustained high-speed lateral loading through Maggotts-Becketts-Chapel is some of the hardest cornering load tyres see anywhere on the calendar. Silverstone also has the highest rain_race_frequency among the "permanent circuit" group (≈0.25) — British summer weather is famously unpredictable, and a wet or mixed-conditions Silverstone race is a regular occurrence rather than a rare event, which is part of why practice and qualifying pace here can be a noisier predictor of race-day pace than at a more climatically stable venue.

Safety car frequency (≈0.3) and DNF rate (≈0.1) are both moderate — the circuit has generous, modern run-off in most places, but the sheer cornering speed means the corners that do punish a mistake tend to do so decisively.
