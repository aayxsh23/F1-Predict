Red Bull Ring (Spielberg) — Circuit Notes

The Red Bull Ring is one of the shortest permanent lap distances on the calendar (4.318 km) set in the Styrian hills, with dramatic elevation change and just three real braking zones per lap (the lowest braking-zone count in the reference table) — most of the circuit is flowing, high-speed corners connected by short straights. That layout keeps overtaking difficulty moderate (≈0.4): the three hard-braking zones are all genuine passing opportunities, but there's comparatively little of the lap where a pass can actually be attempted.

Tyre degradation is on the higher side (≈3/5) for a circuit this short, since the fast, sweeping corners put sustained load through the tyres despite the low overall lap distance. Safety car frequency (≈0.3) and DNF rate (≈0.1) are both fairly average, and rain is a moderate factor (≈0.1) given the circuit's mountainous setting, where weather can change quickly within a race weekend.

Because the lap is so short, drivers spend a larger fraction of total race distance in genuinely high-speed, low-margin-for-error corners than at a longer circuit — small qualifying-pace deficits tend to compound less dramatically over a race distance here than at a circuit with more low-speed technical sections to lose time in.
