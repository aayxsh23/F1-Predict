Miami International Autodrome — Circuit Notes

Miami is a purpose-built temporary circuit around the Hard Rock Stadium campus — classified as a street circuit (is_street_circuit = 1) even though, like Melbourne, it's wider and more permanent-feeling than a genuine downtown street track. Overtaking difficulty is moderate (≈0.5): there's a long back straight into a hard-braking zone that creates a real passing opportunity, offset by several tighter, wall-lined sections earlier in the lap where a mistake is costly.

Safety car frequency is on the higher side (≈0.6), consistent with most temporary/street-adjacent circuits — walls close to the racing line mean incidents are more likely to require a full safety car rather than resolving themselves. DNF rate (≈0.12) and tyre degradation (≈3/5) are both fairly typical mid-pack values. Rain is a minor factor (≈0.05) given the race's typical early-May date and South Florida climate, though it is not negligible the way it is at the Gulf-region rounds.

As with Jeddah, being a newer temporary layout means track evolution across the weekend (rubber laid down, grip building session to session) is more pronounced than at a permanent circuit with a stable, long-established surface.
